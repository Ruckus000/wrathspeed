## Strength — repetitions (17)
Counted in reps. `TOO HARD? DO THIS` is a real regression for all of these.

### `bw-squat` — Bodyweight squat
*Cue:* Sit back, knees track over toes, stand tall.

**How to do it**
1. Stand with your feet about shoulder-width apart, toes turned out slightly.
2. Push your hips back and bend your knees, letting them travel out over your toes. Chest stays up.
3. Go as low as you can with your heels flat and your lower back neutral, then drive through the floor to stand tall.

**Should feel** — Work through the thighs and glutes. Your heels stay down the whole time.

**Common mistake** — Knees collapsing inward, or heels lifting near the bottom. The knees caving usually means you have gone deeper than you control. Heels lifting is more often stiff ankles -- put a book under each heel and see if the depth comes back.

**Too hard? Do this** — Sit down to a chair or bench and stand back up. Same movement, less depth.

### `split-squat` — Split squat
*Cue:* Short stance, drop the back knee, keep the front heel down.

**How to do it**
1. Step one foot forward into a short stance, feet about hip-width apart side to side.
2. Lower straight down until the back knee is just off the floor, front heel flat.
3. Drive through the front foot to stand. Finish all reps, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The front thigh and glute working, with a stretch across the front of the back hip.

**Common mistake** — A stance so long it becomes a lunge, or the front heel lifting as you descend.

**Too hard? Do this** — Hold a wall or doorframe for balance, and stop the descent higher.

### `reverse-lunge` — Reverse lunge
*Cue:* Step back, both knees near 90 degrees, push through the front foot.

**How to do it**
1. Stand tall, then step one foot straight back.
2. Lower until both knees are near 90 degrees, torso upright.
3. Push through the front foot to return to standing, then alternate sides.

**Should feel** — The front leg doing the work. The back leg is mostly there for balance.

**Common mistake** — Leaning the chest out over the front knee, or letting that knee drift inward.

**Too hard? Do this** — Hold a wall for balance, or take a shorter step back.

### `glute-bridge` — Glute bridge
*Cue:* Ribs down, squeeze glutes at the top, don't overarch.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and about hip-width apart.
2. Press through your heels and lift your hips until your body makes a straight line from knees to shoulders.
3. Squeeze the glutes at the top, then lower with control.

**Should feel** — Glutes and hamstrings. You should not feel this in your lower back.

**Common mistake** — Arching the low back to get higher. The height comes from the hips finishing, not the spine bending.

### `single-leg-bridge` — Single-leg glute bridge
*Cue:* One foot planted, hips square, pause at the top.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your back with one foot planted close to your glutes, the other leg lifted.
2. Drive through the planted heel to lift your hips, keeping both hip bones level.
3. Pause at the top, then lower with control. Finish all reps on one side before switching.

**Should feel** — The glute of the planted leg doing the work.

**Common mistake** — Letting the lifted side drop so the hips twist, or pushing through the toes instead of the heel.

**Too hard? Do this** — Do a two-leg glute bridge, or rest the lifted foot lightly on the floor for support.

### `calf-raise` — Calf raise
*Cue:* Slow lower, full range, equal work left and right.

**How to do it**
1. Stand tall with your feet hip-width and your weight through the balls of the feet.
2. Rise as high as you can onto your toes.
3. Lower slowly, all the way down, before starting the next rep.
4. Do some sets with the knee slightly bent. That shifts the work onto the deeper calf muscle, which is the one doing most of the work when you run.

**Should feel** — The calves through a long range. The slow lower is where most of the work is.

**Common mistake** — Bouncing at the bottom and cutting the range short at both ends.

**Too hard? Do this** — Hold a wall for balance and keep both feet on the floor.

### `dead-bug` — Dead bug
*Cue:* Low back stays heavy on the floor as opposite arm and leg move.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your back with your arms straight up and your knees over your hips, bent at 90 degrees.
2. Flatten your low back gently against the floor and keep that same position for every rep -- you are holding it still, not pressing it hard.
3. Slowly lower one arm overhead and the opposite leg toward the floor, then return and alternate.

**Should feel** — A deep brace across the abdominals, hardest when the arm and leg reach furthest.

**Common mistake** — Letting the low back arch off the floor as the leg lowers. That is the moment the exercise stops working.

**Too hard? Do this** — Move the legs only, or lower them a shorter distance.

### `bird-dog` — Bird dog
*Cue:* Reach long, keep hips level, pause, switch.

**How to do it**
1. Start on hands and knees, hands under shoulders and knees under hips.
2. Reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back until both are level with your torso.
3. Pause, return with control, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The back of the shoulder, the glute of the reaching leg, and a brace through the middle holding you still.

**Common mistake** — Letting the hips tip as the leg lifts, or lifting the leg above hip height and arching the back.

**Too hard? Do this** — Move one limb at a time -- arm only, then leg only.

### `push-up` — Push-up
*Cue:* Body plank-stiff, elbows ~45 degrees, chest to a fist off the floor.

**How to do it**
1. Hands under your shoulders, body plank-stiff from head to heels.
2. Lower until your chest is about a fist's height off the floor, elbows tracking back at roughly 45 degrees.
3. Push the floor away and return to the top without letting your hips sag.

**Should feel** — Chest, shoulders and triceps working, midsection braced throughout.

**Common mistake** — Hips sagging toward the floor, or elbows flaring straight out to the sides.

**Too hard? Do this** — Put your hands on a bench, a step, or a wall. The more upright you are, the easier it gets.

### `band-row` — Band row
*Cue:* Pull elbows back, squeeze shoulder blades, don't shrug.

**How to do it**
1. Anchor the band at chest height and hold an end in each hand with your arms straight.
2. Pull your elbows back past your ribs, squeezing the shoulder blades together.
3. Return slowly, keeping tension in the band the whole way.

**Should feel** — The mid-back between the shoulder blades, with the shoulders staying down.

**Common mistake** — Shrugging the shoulders toward the ears, or pulling with the hands so the elbows flare wide.

**Too hard? Do this** — Step closer to the anchor, or use a lighter band.

### `db-row` — Dumbbell row
*Cue:* Hinge, back flat, pull the weight to the hip.

**How to do it**
1. Hinge at the hips with a flat back, one hand supported on a bench or your thigh.
2. Let the weight hang, then pull it to your hip with the elbow close to your body.
3. Lower under control. Finish the side before switching.

**Should feel** — The lat down the side of the back, and the mid-back at the top of the pull.

**Common mistake** — Twisting the torso to lift the weight higher, or letting the back round as it lowers.

**Too hard? Do this** — Go lighter and slow the lowering down.

### `goblet-squat` — Goblet squat
*Cue:* Hold the weight at the chest, sit between the heels.

**How to do it**
1. Hold one weight against your chest with both hands, elbows tucked in.
2. Sit down between your heels, keeping your chest tall and your heels flat.
3. Drive through the floor to stand.

**Should feel** — Thighs and glutes, with the upper back working to keep the chest up.

**Common mistake** — The chest falling forward as you descend, which turns it into a hinge rather than a squat.

**Too hard? Do this** — Go lighter, or squat down to a box and stand back up.

### `rdl` — Romanian deadlift
*Cue:* Soft knees, push hips back, feel the hamstrings, stand tall.

**How to do it**
1. Stand tall holding the weight in front of your thighs, knees softly bent.
2. Push your hips straight back and let the weight slide down your legs, back flat.
3. Stop when you feel a strong stretch in the hamstrings, then drive your hips forward to stand tall.

**Should feel** — A clear stretch down the back of the thighs on the way down, glutes at the top.

**Common mistake** — Rounding the lower back, or turning it into a squat. The knee angle barely changes through the whole rep.

**Too hard? Do this** — Go lighter, or with no weight at all, and shorten the range — stop halfway down.

### `step-up` — Step-up
*Cue:* Whole foot on the box, drive up, control the lower.

**How to do it**
1. Place one whole foot on the box -- the heel must be on it, not hanging off the edge.
2. Drive through that foot to stand up on the box, without pushing off the trailing foot.
3. Lower slowly under control. Finish the side, then switch.

**Should feel** — The stepping leg's thigh and glute. The other leg should feel like a passenger.

**Common mistake** — Pushing off the back foot to get up, which lets the working leg do far less than it looks.

**Too hard? Do this** — Use a lower box.

### `hip-thrust` — Hip thrust
*Cue:* Upper back on the bench, chin tucked, squeeze at lockout.

**How to do it**
1. Sit on the floor with your upper back against a bench, feet flat and about hip-width apart.
2. Drive through your heels to lift your hips until your torso is parallel with the floor.
3. Squeeze the glutes at the top with your chin tucked, then lower with control.

**Should feel** — The glutes, hard, at the top of every rep.

**Common mistake** — Arching the low back and lifting the ribs at the top instead of finishing with the glutes.

**Too hard? Do this** — Do the same movement from the floor, without the bench.

### `pull-up` — Pull-up or hang hold
*Cue:* Shoulders down and back. Use a hang if pull-ups are not ready.

**How to do it**
1. Hang from the bar with your hands a little wider than your shoulders.
2. Pull your shoulders down and back first, then drive your elbows toward your ribs until your chin passes the bar.
3. Lower all the way to a full hang under control.

**Should feel** — Lats and upper back. The arms help, but should not be doing all of it.

**Common mistake** — Kicking the legs to generate swing, or stopping short of a full hang between reps.

**Too hard? Do this** — Hold a dead hang for time, or jump to the top and lower yourself slowly.

### `swiss-crunch` — Swiss ball crunch
*Cue:* Hips stay still, curl the ribs toward the pelvis.

**How to do it**
1. Sit on the ball and walk your feet out until your low back is supported on it.
2. Curl your ribs toward your pelvis, lifting your shoulders a short way.
3. Lower back over the ball with control.

**Should feel** — The abdominals through a longer range than a floor crunch, because the ball lets you start extended.

**Common mistake** — Pulling on the back of your neck, or moving at the hips instead of curling the spine.

**Too hard? Do this** — Extend back over the ball a shorter way, and cross your arms on your chest. If your back is sensitive to bending, skip this one -- the plank and dead bug cover what a runner needs.

## Strength — holds (3)
Timed, not repped. There is no rep to count, so `SHOULD FEEL` carries most of the teaching.

### `side-plank` — Side plank · 30s hold
*Cue:* Hips stacked, body in a straight line, breathe.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your side with your forearm under your shoulder and your feet stacked.
2. Lift your hips until your body makes one straight line from head to heels.
3. Hold, breathing normally, then repeat on the other side.

**Should feel** — The side of your waist nearest the floor, and the shoulder holding you up.

**Common mistake** — Hips sagging toward the floor, or the chest rolling forward so you are no longer on your side.

**Too hard? Do this** — Bend your knees and hold from the knees rather than the feet.

### `farmer-carry` — Farmer carry · 40s hold
*Cue:* Tall posture, ribs stacked, walk slow and even.

**How to do it**
1. Pick up a weight in each hand and stand tall with your ribs stacked over your hips.
2. Walk at a steady, even pace with your shoulders down and the weights quiet.
3. Keep walking for the full time, then set the weights down under control.

**Should feel** — Grip, shoulders, and a constant brace through the whole midsection.

**Common mistake** — Leaning back to counter the load, or letting the weights swing so the torso twists with them.

**Too hard? Do this** — Go lighter, or carry one weight at a time and swap hands halfway.

### `plank` — Front plank · 45s hold
*Cue:* Squeeze glutes, push the floor away, breathe.

**How to do it**
1. Set your forearms on the floor under your shoulders, elbows bent about 90 degrees.
2. Step your feet back until your body makes one straight line from head to heels.
3. Squeeze your glutes, push the floor away with your forearms, and breathe normally for the whole hold.

**Should feel** — A deep brace across the entire midsection. Shoulders and glutes should be working too.

**Common mistake** — Hips drifting up into a pike, or sagging toward the floor. Both take the work off the abdominals.

**Too hard? Do this** — Drop to your knees, or set your forearms on a bench so your body sits at an angle.

## Warm-up mobility (10)
Range and control rather than effort. Several have no meaningful regression, so `TOO HARD? DO THIS` is absent.

### `arm-circles` — Arm circles
*Cue:* Small circles first, then wider. Half forward, half back.

**How to do it**
1. Stand tall with your arms out to the sides at shoulder height.
2. Draw small circles, letting them grow as the shoulders loosen.
3. Spend half the time circling forward and half circling back.

**Should feel** — The shoulders warming and moving more freely as the circles grow.

**Common mistake** — Starting at full size, or shrugging the shoulders up toward the ears.

### `cat-cow` — Cat-cow
*Cue:* On all fours. Round the spine, then arch. Move with your breath.

**How to do it**
1. Start on hands and knees, hands under shoulders and knees under hips.
2. Exhale and round your spine, tucking the tailbone and letting the head drop.
3. Inhale and reverse it -- chest forward, tailbone up. Keep moving with the breath.

**Should feel** — The spine moving segment by segment, not just hinging at the low back.

**Common mistake** — Rushing it, or moving only the low back while the upper back stays flat.

### `standing-pelvic-tilt` — Standing pelvic tilt
*Cue:* Tuck the tailbone under, then release. Small range, ribs quiet.

**How to do it**
1. Stand with your feet hip-width apart and your knees soft.
2. Tuck your tailbone under, flattening the low back slightly.
3. Release back to neutral. Keep the movement small and the ribs still.

**Should feel** — A small, controlled movement low in the back and around the hips.

**Common mistake** — Making it big, or moving the ribs and shoulders instead of the pelvis.

### `hip-circles` — Standing hip circles
*Cue:* Hands on hips, draw big slow circles. Both directions.

**How to do it**
1. Stand with your feet a little wider than your hips and your hands on your hips.
2. Draw a slow circle with the hips, keeping the feet planted and the head fairly still.
3. Do half the time in each direction.

**Should feel** — The hips travelling through their full range, loosening as the circles continue.

**Common mistake** — Rushing, or swinging the whole upper body instead of moving at the hips.

### `ankle-circles` — Ankle circles
*Cue:* Lift one foot, circle the ankle slowly. Both directions, both sides.

**How to do it**
1. Stand on one leg, holding a wall if you need to, and lift the other foot off the floor.
2. Circle the ankle slowly through as much range as it has.
3. Change direction halfway, then swap feet.

**Should feel** — The ankle moving freely. Nothing should pinch at the front of the joint.

**Common mistake** — Going fast and small. The point is range, and only slow circles find it.

**Too hard? Do this** — Sit down and rest the ankle across the opposite knee, so balance is not part of it.

### `leg-swings-front` — Front leg swings
*Cue:* Hold a wall. Swing the leg front to back, relaxed, growing range.

**How to do it**
1. Stand side-on to a wall and rest a hand on it.
2. Swing the outside leg forward and back, keeping it relaxed and your torso tall.
3. Let the range grow over the first few swings rather than forcing it, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The hamstring and the front of the hip loosening as the swing gets longer.

**Common mistake** — Forcing the height early, or arching the low back to swing the leg higher.

### `leg-swings-side` — Lateral leg swings
*Cue:* Face the wall. Swing the leg across the body and out. Hips square.

**How to do it**
1. Face the wall with both hands resting on it.
2. Swing one leg across the front of your body, then out to the side.
3. Keep both hips facing the wall throughout, then switch legs.

**Should feel** — The inner thigh on the way across, and the outside of the hip on the way out.

**Common mistake** — Letting the hips rotate to get more range, which takes the work out of the leg you are swinging.

### `groiners` — Groiners
*Cue:* From a push-up position, step a foot outside the hand. Alternate.

**How to do it**
1. Start in a push-up position on your hands, body in a straight line.
2. Step one foot forward and plant it flat on the floor outside the hand on that side.
3. Return it to the plank position and alternate sides.

**Should feel** — The groin and deep hip of the leg you stepped forward, and the front of the hip of the leg left behind.

**Common mistake** — Driving the knee under your chest instead of planting the foot outside the hand. That is a mountain climber, which is a different exercise.

**Too hard? Do this** — Step the foot as far forward as it goes and help it the rest of the way with your hand, or lower the back knee to the floor.

### `worlds-greatest-stretch` — World's greatest stretch
*Cue:* Lunge, forearm to the floor inside the foot, then rotate the top arm up.

**How to do it**
1. Step into a deep lunge and place both hands on the floor.
2. Drop the inside forearm toward the floor beside your front foot.
3. Rotate the other arm up toward the ceiling, following your hand with your eyes. Return, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The front hip and groin, and a rotation through the upper back.

**Common mistake** — Rushing the rotation before the forearm has settled, so the twist comes from the low back rather than the upper back.

**Too hard? Do this** — Keep both hands on the floor and skip the rotation, or rest the back knee down.

### `inchworm` — Inchworm
*Cue:* Hinge, walk the hands out to a plank, walk the feet in. Keep legs long.

**How to do it**
1. Stand tall, hinge at the hips and put your hands on the floor. Bend the knees as much as you need to -- this is a warm-up, not a hamstring stretch.
2. Walk your hands out until you are in a plank.
3. Walk your feet toward your hands with the legs as long as you can manage, then stand up.

**Should feel** — The hamstrings on the way in, and a brace through the middle at the plank.

**Common mistake** — Letting the hips sag at the plank, or bending the knees so much on the walk in that the hamstrings do nothing.

**Too hard? Do this** — Bend the knees as much as you need to on the way down and on the way in.

## Running drills (12)
The mechanic and its purpose. `COMMON MISTAKE` here names the drill each one gets confused with — the same wrong matches recorded in `exercise-drill-illustration-brief.md` while sourcing demo clips.

### `arm-drill` — Kneeling arm drill
*Cue:* Tall kneeling. Drive elbows back, hands cheek to pocket, relaxed hands.

**How to do it**
1. Kneel tall on both knees with your hips underneath you and your legs still.
2. Drive one elbow back while the other hand comes forward, keeping both elbows bent.
3. Let each hand travel from cheek to pocket, hands relaxed, then build the speed up.

**Should feel** — The shoulders working while the torso stays quiet. The legs do nothing at all.

**Common mistake** — A small pumping motion in front of the torso. The amplitude is the drill -- hand to cheek, hand to pocket. That is the drill's range, not your easy-run range.

**Too hard? Do this** — Slow it right down and find the full range first. Speed comes after.

### `wall-drill` — Acceleration wall drill
*Cue:* Lean into the wall from the ankles. Drive one knee up, punch the foot down.

**How to do it**
1. Lean into a wall with straight arms and your body in one line from head to heels, leaning from the ankles.
2. Drive one knee up to hip height.
3. Punch that foot back down to the floor underneath your hip, then switch legs.

**Should feel** — The lean held by the whole body, and a sharp contact each time the foot strikes.

**Common mistake** — Bending at the hips so the body folds, which loses the forward lean the drill exists to teach.

**Too hard? Do this** — Hold the lean without moving your legs, then add one knee drive at a time.

### `claw-series` — Moving claw series
*Cue:* Reach the foot forward, then paw it back under the hip. Quick ground contact.

**How to do it**
1. Travel forward at an easy jog.
2. Reach one foot forward -- a comfortable reach, not the furthest you can get it.
3. Sweep it actively backward before it lands, so it strikes the ground under your hip rather than in front of it.

**Should feel** — The hamstring and glute pulling the foot back just before it lands.

**Common mistake** — Letting the foot land where you reached it. The backward sweep before contact is the whole drill. Save this one for when you are properly warm, and skip it if a hamstring is sore or recently strained -- this is the position hamstrings tear in.

**Too hard? Do this** — Do it walking first, exaggerating the backward sweep.

### `butt-kicks` — Butt kicks
*Cue:* Heels to glutes, knees pointing down, quick light cadence.

**How to do it**
1. Jog on the spot or travel slowly forward.
2. Flick each heel up to your glute, keeping the knee pointing down at the floor.
3. Keep the contacts quick and light rather than reaching for height.

**Should feel** — A quick, light rhythm with the heels flicking close behind you. It should feel bouncy rather than effortful.

**Common mistake** — Letting the knees swing forward, which turns it into a high-knee run, or landing heavily.

**Too hard? Do this** — Slow the cadence and take a smaller heel lift.

### `single-leg-butt-kick` — Single-leg butt kick
*Cue:* One leg cycles, the other stays quiet. Stay tall through the hips.

**How to do it**
1. Jog easily forward.
2. Flick one heel to the glute repeatedly while the other leg keeps a normal running rhythm.
3. Stay tall through the hips, then switch to the other side.

**Should feel** — One hamstring doing repeated quick work while the rest of the stride carries on as normal.

**Common mistake** — Alternating both legs. That is the standard butt kick and a different drill -- here only one leg cycles.

**Too hard? Do this** — Do it on the spot before adding forward travel.

### `fast-skipping` — Fast skipping
*Cue:* Skip for height off a stiff ankle. Opposite arm drives with the knee.

**How to do it**
1. Skip forward, taking two contacts on one foot before switching.
2. Get the height from a stiff, springy ankle rather than a deep knee bend.
3. Drive the opposite arm with the lead knee.

**Should feel** — Springy calves and ankles, with the time on the ground feeling short.

**Common mistake** — Turning it into an A-skip with a big knee lift and a soft, deep bend. The bounce comes from the ankle.

**Too hard? Do this** — Skip on the spot with a smaller bounce until the rhythm is there.

### `carioca` — Carioca
*Cue:* Travel sideways, trail leg crossing front then behind. Let the hips rotate.

**How to do it**
1. Travel sideways with your arms out to the sides for balance.
2. Cross the trail leg in front of the lead leg, step, then cross it behind.
3. Let the hips rotate freely with each crossing, and cover the same distance back the other way.

**Should feel** — The hips rotating, and the whole thing smoothing out once the rhythm arrives.

**Common mistake** — Only crossing in front and shuffling back. Both crossings -- in front and behind -- are the drill.

**Too hard? Do this** — Walk it slowly until the crossing pattern is automatic.

### `lateral-bound` — Lateral bound
*Cue:* Push off one leg, land soft on the other, hold a beat before rebounding.

**How to do it**
1. Stand on one leg, then push sideways off it.
2. Land on the other leg and absorb the landing softly.
3. Hold that landing for a beat before pushing back the other way.

**Should feel** — The glute of the landing leg controlling you, then producing the next push. Stop the set if the landings turn loud or heavy, or anything starts to hurt.

**Common mistake** — Rebounding immediately. The pause is what trains the landing, and the landing is the point.

**Too hard? Do this** — Shorten the distance and step rather than bound.

### `diagonal-bound` — Alternate leg diagonal bound
*Cue:* Long diagonal bounds, alternating legs. Cover ground, land quietly.

**How to do it**
1. Push off one leg and travel forward and across, at a diagonal.
2. Land on the opposite leg, absorbing quietly.
3. Push straight off into the next bound, alternating legs and driving the opposite arm.

**Should feel** — A long moment of flight, with the glute absorbing each landing. Stop the set if the landings turn loud or heavy, or anything starts to hurt.

**Common mistake** — Bounding straight out sideways. That is a lateral bound; this one travels forward and across.

**Too hard? Do this** — Shorten the bounds, then add distance as the landings get quiet.

### `split-jump` — Split jump
*Cue:* Lunge, jump, switch legs in the air, absorb the landing.

**How to do it**
1. Drop into a lunge with both knees bent.
2. Jump up and switch your legs in the air.
3. Land in a lunge on the other side, absorbing softly through both legs.

**Should feel** — Thighs and glutes on take-off, with both legs sharing the landing. Stop the set if the landings turn loud or heavy, or anything starts to hurt.

**Common mistake** — Landing stiff-legged and loud, or letting the front knee collapse inward as you land.

**Too hard? Do this** — Step through from one lunge to the other instead of jumping, or reduce the depth.

### `knee-tuck-jump` — Knee tuck jump
*Cue:* Jump and pull both knees to the chest. Land soft, reset, repeat.

**How to do it**
1. Stand tall with your feet hip-width apart.
2. Jump and pull both knees toward your chest.
3. Land softly through the whole foot, stand up, and reset before the next one.

**Should feel** — An explosive jump followed by a quiet, controlled landing. Stop the set if the landings turn loud or heavy, or anything starts to hurt.

**Common mistake** — Rushing straight into the next jump. Each rep starts from a settled stand.

**Too hard? Do this** — Jump without the tuck, or tuck the knees to a lower height.

### `hurdle-hops` — Hurdle hops
*Cue:* Hop over low obstacles off both feet. Stiff ankles, minimal ground time.

**How to do it**
1. Set out a row of at least three low markers -- lines on the ground, a rolled towel, anything that gives way if you clip it. Nothing rigid enough to catch a tired foot.
2. Hop over them with both feet together, one after another without stopping.
3. Keep the ankles stiff and the ground contact between hurdles as short as you can.

**Should feel** — Springy ankles, and very little time spent on the floor. Stop the set if the landings turn loud or heavy, or anything starts to hurt.

**Common mistake** — One hop over one hurdle with a reset in between, or jumping down from a box. It is a continuous row.

**Too hard? Do this** — Lower the obstacles, or use lines on the ground instead of hurdles.

## Static stretches (12)
`SHOULD FEEL` is the load-bearing field: where you feel it is what tells you the set-up is right.

### `calf-stretch-wall` — Wall calf stretch
*Cue:* Back leg straight, heel down, hips forward. Hold and breathe.

**How to do it**
1. Stand facing a wall with your hands on it and one foot well back.
2. Keep the back leg straight and press the back heel down into the floor.
3. Ease your hips forward until you feel the stretch, and hold. Then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The thick upper part of the back calf.

**Common mistake** — Letting the back heel lift, which removes the stretch entirely.

**Too hard? Do this** — Bring the back foot closer to the wall.

### `soleus-achilles` — Soleus and achilles stretch
*Cue:* Same as the calf stretch but bend the back knee. Lower and deeper.

**How to do it**
1. Set up as for the wall calf stretch, with the back foot a little closer in.
2. Keep the heel pressed down and bend the back knee.
3. Sink down rather than forward, and hold. Then switch sides.

**Should feel** — Lower than the straight-leg version -- down near the achilles and the base of the calf.

**Common mistake** — Bending the knee but letting the heel come up, which loses the stretch.

**Too hard? Do this** — Bend the knee less, and bring the foot in closer.

### `runners-stretch` — Runner's stretch
*Cue:* Deep lunge, hands framing the front foot, sink the back hip down.

**How to do it**
1. Step into a deep lunge and place both hands on the floor either side of your front foot.
2. Let the back hip sink toward the floor.
3. Hold, breathing, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The front of the back hip, and a stretch deep in the front groin.

**Common mistake** — Leaving the back hip high, which turns it into a hamstring stretch instead.

**Too hard? Do this** — Rest the back knee on the floor, and put your hands on a block or a low step.

### `seated-hamstring` — Seated hamstring stretch
*Cue:* Hinge from the hips, not the spine. Chest toward the thigh.

**How to do it**
1. Sit with one leg straight out in front and the other bent or tucked in.
2. Hinge forward from the hips with a long spine.
3. Take your chest toward the thigh, stop where it bites, and hold. Then switch sides.

**Should feel** — Along the back of the straight leg's thigh.

**Common mistake** — Rounding the back to reach the foot. That drags the pull into the low back and onto the nerve rather than the muscle. If you feel tingling, or a stretch running down into the calf or foot, back off and bend the knee a little.

**Too hard? Do this** — Sit on a cushion, and let the straight leg bend slightly.

### `kneeling-hip-flexor` — Kneeling hip flexor stretch
*Cue:* Half kneeling, tuck the tailbone, then ease the hips forward.

**How to do it**
1. Kneel on one knee with the other foot flat on the floor in front of you.
2. Tuck your tailbone under first -- this is what makes the stretch work.
3. Then ease your hips forward a small way and hold. Switch sides.

**Should feel** — Across the front of the hip on the kneeling side.

**Common mistake** — Pushing the hips forward without the tuck, which arches the low back and stretches that instead.

**Too hard? Do this** — Put a folded towel under the knee and shift forward a shorter distance.

### `quad-stretch` — Standing quad stretch
*Cue:* Heel to glute, knees together, stand tall. Don't arch the low back.

**How to do it**
1. Stand tall, holding a wall if you need to.
2. Take one heel to your glute and hold the ankle.
3. Keep the knees together and the tailbone tucked, then hold. Switch sides.

**Should feel** — The front of the thigh, and a little across the front of the hip.

**Common mistake** — Letting the knee drift out to the side, or the low back arch. Both take the stretch off the quad.

**Too hard? Do this** — Loop a strap or a towel around the ankle, or do it lying on your side.

### `it-band-glute` — Glute and outer hip stretch
*Cue:* Cross the top leg over, hug the knee across the body.

**How to do it**
1. Sit or lie with one leg crossed over the other.
2. Draw the top knee across your body toward the opposite shoulder.
3. Hold where it bites, breathing, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The outside of the hip and the glute of the crossed leg.

**Common mistake** — Pulling hard enough to twist the shoulders round, which moves the stretch into the low back.

**Too hard? Do this** — Pull it a shorter distance across, and keep the bottom leg bent.

### `lying-glute` — Lying glute stretch
*Cue:* Ankle over the opposite knee, pull the bottom thigh toward you.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your back with both knees bent.
2. Cross one ankle over the opposite knee to make a figure four.
3. Reach through and pull the bottom thigh toward your chest. Hold, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — Deep in the glute of the crossed leg.

**Common mistake** — Pulling on the crossed knee rather than the bottom thigh, which twists that knee instead of stretching the glute.

**Too hard? Do this** — Do it seated in a chair, leaning gently forward instead of pulling.

### `lying-crossover` — Lying spinal crossover
*Cue:* On your back, drop one knee across the body. Shoulders stay down.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your back with your arms out to the sides.
2. Bring one knee up and let it fall across your body toward the floor.
3. Keep both shoulders on the floor and turn your head the other way. Switch sides.

**Should feel** — A gentle twist through the hips and the middle of the back, and a stretch across the outside of the hip. Keep it easy -- this is not a position to push into.

**Common mistake** — Letting the opposite shoulder lift to get the knee lower. The shoulder staying down is what creates the twist.

**Too hard? Do this** — Put a cushion under the falling knee so it has less distance to travel.

### `side-lying-groin` — Side-lying groin stretch
*Cue:* Open the top leg and let it settle. Relax rather than force it.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your side with the bottom leg bent for support.
2. Let the top leg open away from the bottom one.
3. Hold the leg open and breathe -- this one takes effort to hold. Then switch sides.

**Should feel** — The inner thigh of the top leg, working as you hold it open.

**Common mistake** — Shoving the leg further open with your hand. Let the leg hold itself where it can.

**Too hard? Do this** — Bend the top knee. That shortens the lever, so the leg is easier to hold up.

### `hug-knees-to-chest` — Knees to chest
*Cue:* Hug both knees in, rock gently side to side.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your back and draw both knees toward your chest.
2. Wrap your arms around them and hold.
3. Rock gently from side to side.

**Should feel** — The low back opening across its whole width.

**Common mistake** — Pulling hard enough to lift your head and neck off the floor.

### `childs-pose` — Child's pose
*Cue:* Hips to heels, arms long, let the ribs drop and breathe.

**How to do it**
1. Kneel and sit your hips back toward your heels.
2. Reach your arms long in front and let your chest sink toward the floor.
3. Breathe into your back and let the ribs settle.

**Should feel** — The low back and the shoulders lengthening as you breathe.

**Common mistake** — Holding tension in the shoulders instead of letting the chest sink.

**Too hard? Do this** — Widen the knees, or put a cushion between your hips and your heels. If the knees complain, use the cushion from the start.

## Foam rolling (4)
`COMMON MISTAKE` carries the safety note here, because the risk is joint pressure rather than bad form.

### `calves-smr` — Calf roll
*Cue:* Roll slowly, pause on tender spots for a few breaths.

**How to do it**
1. Sit with the roller under one calf and your hands on the floor behind you.
2. Roll slowly from the ankle to just below the knee.
3. When you find a tender spot, stop there and breathe until it eases. Then switch legs.

**Should feel** — Firm pressure -- uncomfortable, but not sharp. If it stings or shoots, or you get pins and needles, come off it and move on.

**Common mistake** — Rolling fast. Speed feels productive and does very little; the pauses are what work.

**Too hard? Do this** — Keep the other foot on the floor to carry some of your weight.

### `hamstring-smr` — Hamstring roll
*Cue:* Roll hip to knee. Slow. Stop where it bites and breathe.

**How to do it**
1. Sit with the roller under one hamstring and your hands behind you for support.
2. Roll slowly between the hip and a point just above the knee.
3. Pause on tender spots and breathe until they ease. Then switch legs.

**Should feel** — Firm pressure through the back of the thigh -- uncomfortable, but not sharp. If it stings or shoots, or you get pins and needles, come off it and move on.

**Common mistake** — Rolling right over the back of the knee. Stay between the hip and just above it.

**Too hard? Do this** — Put the other foot on the floor to take some weight off the roller.

### `it-band-smr` — IT band roll
*Cue:* Side-lying on the roller, hip to just above the knee. Never on the knee.

**How to do it**
1. Lie on your side with the roller under the outside of your thigh.
2. Support yourself on your forearm, with your top foot on the floor.
3. Roll slowly between the hip and a point just above the knee, then switch sides.

**Should feel** — Firm pressure down the outside of the thigh -- uncomfortable, but never sharp. If it stings or shoots, or you get pins and needles, come off it and move on.

**Common mistake** — Grinding the last few inches above the knee. That is exactly where a sore outside-of-knee already hurts, and pressing on it adds to the problem. Spend the time nearer the hip.

**Too hard? Do this** — Take more weight through the top foot and your forearm, so less of you is on the roller. Or roll the muscle around it -- the side of your backside, the front-outside of the thigh -- rather than the band itself.

### `piriformis-smr` — Glute roll
*Cue:* Sit on the roller, ankle over the opposite knee, hunt for the sore spot.

**How to do it**
1. Sit on the roller and cross one ankle over the opposite knee.
2. Lean into the crossed side so your weight settles onto that glute.
3. Move slowly to find the sore spot, then settle on it and breathe for a few breaths. Switch sides.

**Should feel** — Deep in the glute -- a strong, dull ache once you find the spot. If it turns sharp, or anything travels down the leg, come off that spot and move on.

**Common mistake** — Moving constantly. This one is about finding a single spot and staying on it.

**Too hard? Do this** — Keep more weight on your hands and your supporting foot.
